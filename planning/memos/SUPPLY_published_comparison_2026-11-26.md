# Lane SUPPLY's corporate channel against published dynamic scores

2026-11-26. This is the first follow-up lane SUPPLY named
(`planning/lanes/SUPPLY_corporate_capital_channel.md`, "Still open"). It is a
check only, and nothing in the code changes.

**Question.** Does the corporate capital channel's 28% result sit where the
published general-equilibrium scores of a 28% corporate rate sit?

| | This calculator (Biden Corporate 28%, FRB/US-Lite) | Tax Foundation, *Options 3.0*, Option 49 | PWBM, "Options for Raising Corporate Income Tax Rates" (1 Apr 2021), 28% row |
|---|---|---|---|
| Window | FY2026-2035 | 2027-2036 | FY2022-2031 |
| Conventional, 10 yr | -$968.8B | -$1,410.0B (primary) | -$1,028.6B (revenue +$1,028.6B) |
| Revenue lost to feedback | $248.8B (25.7% of conventional), of which $162.2B supply | $403.7B (28.6%) | not published as a dollar figure |
| Output, end of window | -0.29% (FY2035) | not published | **+1.2%** (2031) |
| Output, long run | -0.84% | **-0.5%** | **+2.17%** (2050) |
| Capital stock, long run | not reported | -1.1% | +6.28% (2050) |

**Findings.**
1. **Tax Foundation:** same sign. The share of the conventional score lost to
   feedback is close: 25.7% here against 28.6%. The long-run output effect
   here is about 1.7 times Tax Foundation's (-0.84% against -0.5%), but the
   path converges slowly (λ ≈ 0.044), so by the end of the window only -0.29%
   of it is reached. The ten-year feedback is therefore close to Tax
   Foundation's, and the long-run level is larger.
2. **PWBM:** opposite sign, for a stated reason. In PWBM's OLG model the
   added revenue reduces government debt (-1.44% by 2031, -6.52% by 2050),
   which crowds private capital back in. That outweighs the higher user cost,
   so output and the capital stock both rise. This calculator nets out debt
   service but has no crowding-in channel from lower debt to output, so it
   cannot reproduce that sign. PWBM's 2020 Biden-plan analysis puts the
   domestic capital loss alone at about -0.42% of GDP in 2050. That figure
   comes from PWBM's 2020 Biden business-tax analysis
   (budgetmodel.wharton.upenn.edu/p/2020-06-29-business-taxation-in-the-biden-tax-plan/),
   reported through a search summary and **not yet read in the document**, so
   it should be checked before anyone quotes it.
3. **Verdict:** the channel's in-window feedback agrees with Tax Foundation's
   in size. Its long-run level is at the high end of the published range, and
   it omits the debt crowding-in that reverses PWBM's sign. No change is
   proposed. If the long-run figure is ever shown on the site, it should sit
   beside Tax Foundation's -0.5%, with PWBM's +2.2% and the reason they differ.
